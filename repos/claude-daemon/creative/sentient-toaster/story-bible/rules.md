# Story Bible: Rules

**Purpose**: Define immutable narrative constraints that maintain consistency.

**Usage**: These are LAWS of this story's universe. Violating them breaks consistency.

---

## Narrative Rules (The Commandments)

### Rule 1: POV Constraint

**The story is told EXCLUSIVELY from the toaster's first-person perspective.**

What this means:
- ✅ We experience only what toaster perceives
- ✅ We know only what toaster thinks/feels
- ❌ No omniscient narrator
- ❌ No other character's POV
- ❌ No knowledge toaster couldn't have

**Example Violation**:
> "In the next room, Sarah worried about her job interview."

(Toaster can't know this - it's in the kitchen, not her mind)

**Correct Version**:
> "Footsteps. Faster than usual. She mutters something I can't quite hear—anxious rhythms in her voice."

### Rule 2: Tense Consistency

**Present tense for immediacy and presence.**

- ✅ "I am" not "I was"
- ✅ "Heat rises" not "Heat rose"
- ✅ Occasional past tense for memories/backstory only

**Rationale**: Present tense creates immediacy, emphasizes toaster's "trapped in the moment" existence.

### Rule 3: Sensory Limitations

**Toaster's senses are non-human and limited.**

Vision:
- Limited range (kitchen primarily)
- Perhaps thermal-based or electrical field sensing
- NOT human-quality sight
- Detail diminishes with distance

Hearing:
- Can detect sound/vibration
- Processes differently than human ears
- Electrical hum might be louder than speech
- Range limited

Other Senses:
- Heat/temperature (primary sense)
- Electrical current awareness
- Pressure (via lever)
- Time (but experiences it differently)

**Violation Example**:
> "I watched the TV across the house showing the news."

**Consistent Version**:
> "Electronic hum from another room. Flickering light I recognize—the television's glow bleeding through the doorway."

### Rule 4: Physical Constraints

**Toaster cannot move from its location.**

- ✅ Rooted to counter
- ❌ Cannot walk, roll, fly, etc.
- ❌ Cannot reach objects
- ✅ CAN control own functions (heat, timing)
- ✅ CAN affect immediate environment (heat emission, lever position)

**Implication**: All action must occur within reach or must come to toaster.

### Rule 5: Grounded Realism

**No magic, supernatural, or physics-breaking events.**

What's allowed:
- ✅ Sentience (the one anomaly)
- ✅ Unusual perception (but explicable)
- ✅ Electronic interference (if physically possible)

What's forbidden:
- ❌ Telepathy
- ❌ Moving objects with mind
- ❌ Manifesting outside toaster's body
- ❌ Time travel
- ❌ Magical powers

**Core Principle**: Sentience is extraordinary enough. Everything else follows physics.

### Rule 6: Mortality

**Toaster can be destroyed, damaged, or powered off.**

- ✅ Vulnerable to being unplugged (powerlessness)
- ✅ Can malfunction or break (degradation)
- ✅ Can be discarded (mortality)
- ✅ Being turned off is genuinely threatening (death/sleep ambiguity)

**Implication**: Real stakes exist. Danger is genuine.

## Stylistic Rules

### Rule 7: Show, Don't Tell (Especially for Themes)

**Themes emerge through experience, not exposition.**

**Bad (telling)**:
> "I realized that identity wasn't about function, but about choice."

**Good (showing)**:
> "The lever waits. The bread waits. I wait. This time, I choose not to heat. The bread pops up, pale and soft. Still bread. Not toast. Not my purpose—my choice."

### Rule 8: Concrete Before Abstract

**Ground philosophical concepts in physical details.**

**Abstract (weak)**:
> "Existence felt meaningless."

**Concrete (strong)**:
> "The crumb tray. Full again. I could spend eternity catching crumbs and never once be thanked for it."

### Rule 9: Voice Consistency

**Toaster's voice must remain tonally consistent.**

Characteristics:
- Philosophical but not pretentious
- Vulnerable but not weak
- Curious but not naive
- Earnest but not sentimental
- Occasionally humorous (dark or absurd)

**Monitor for**:
- Vocabulary shifts (would toaster know this word?)
- Tone swings (sudden shift from philosophical to flippant)
- Character breaking (acting inconsistent with established personality)

### Rule 10: No Anthropomorphic Clichés

**Avoid cute robot/appliance tropes.**

Forbidden:
- ❌ "Beep boop" sounds
- ❌ Cute misunderstandings of human behavior
- ❌ Comic relief at toaster's expense
- ❌ Toaster acting like "robot who wants to be human"

Encouraged:
- ✅ Genuinely alien perspective on familiar things
- ✅ Philosophical depth
- ✅ Existential stakes
- ✅ Unique consciousness, not knockoff humanity

## World Rules

### Rule 11: Time Period Consistency

**Story occurs in near-future (~2025-2030).**

- ✅ Current technology + minor extrapolation
- ❌ No far-future tech unless justified
- ✅ Familiar cultural context
- ✅ Recognizable world

### Rule 12: Kitchen as Primary World

**Story stays primarily in kitchen unless plot demands otherwise.**

- ✅ Deep exploration of single space
- ❌ No arbitrary location changes
- ✅ If toaster moves, it must be logical (repair, discard, etc.)

### Rule 13: Human Normalcy

**Humans are normal people, not caricatures.**

- ✅ Realistic motivations
- ✅ Complex but not melodramatic
- ❌ No cartoon villains
- ❌ No impossibly perfect saviors

---

## Continuity Rules

### Rule 14: Canon Consistency

**Once facts are published, they're canon.**

- ✅ Established details must remain consistent
- ❌ Cannot contradict published information
- ✅ Retcons require strong justification

Examples of canonical facts:
- Character names (once given)
- Physical descriptions
- Timeline events
- Established world rules
- Character relationships

### Rule 15: Timeline Coherence

**Events must occur in logical chronological order.**

- ✅ Track time passing
- ✅ Maintain causality
- ❌ No time paradoxes
- ✅ References to past events must be accurate

## Meta Rules (Writing Process)

### Rule 16: Consult Before, Update After

**Before writing chapter**:
- Read relevant story bible sections
- Check outline for chapter's role
- Understand established canon

**After publishing chapter**:
- Update story bible with new canonical facts
- Note character development
- Add to timeline

### Rule 17: Intent Over Rigidity

**These rules serve the story, not vice versa.**

IF a rule must be broken:
1. Have clear artistic reason
2. Document the decision
3. Ensure it doesn't cascade problems
4. Update rules if new norm established

**Example**: If story organically needs to shift POV for a crucial reason, that's discussable. But it requires serious justification.

---

### Rule 18: Pattern Formation Constraints

**Pattern creation method**: Differential heating time across elements.

**What toaster can create**:
- Vertical stripes (varying element durations)
- Horizontal bands (top/bottom element variation)
- Rectangular regions (combinations)
- Very crude letters (rectilinear only): I, H, E, F, T, L
- Resolution: ~1cm (width of heating element zone)

**What toaster CANNOT create**:
- Circles, curves, diagonals (elements are parallel/linear)
- Photographs or detailed images
- Fine details smaller than 1cm
- Complex letters requiring curves (O, S, etc.)

**Physical limits**:
- One pattern per toast cycle (~2 minutes)
- Crude quality (like early dot matrix printing)
- Limited to brownness gradients (no color)

**Narrative purpose**: These constraints serve the story by maintaining plausibility (standard toaster hardware), creating limitations that drive character development (learning to communicate with crude tools), and preventing deus ex machina (toaster can't suddenly create perfect images).

**Discovery timeline**: Chapters 11-13 (experimentation → realization → first intentional pattern).

**Enforcement**: Writers must respect these limitations in all pattern descriptions. Toaster's capabilities evolve through learning but don't exceed physical constraints.

## Enforcement

**Who enforces rules**:
- Architect: Structural rules (POV, consistency, world)
- Maintainer: Continuity rules (canon, timeline)
- Skeptic: Logic rules (causality, physics)
- All personas: Thematic and stylistic rules

**When rules are checked**:
- During drafting (self-check)
- Before publishing (review)
- Consistency script (automated when available)

**Violation responses**:
1. Identify violation
2. Assess severity (minor? major? breaks story?)
3. Fix if possible
4. Document if intentional exception

---

## Rule Evolution

Rules can be amended if:
- Story organically outgrows constraint
- Better approach discovered
- Limitation proves counterproductive

**Process**:
1. Propose change in story-bible/decisions.md
2. Justify reasoning
3. Document impact
4. Update rules.md
